Prologue

In my younger and more vulnerable years, my father gave me some advice that I have been turning over in my mind ever since. I didn't take it.

Instead, I went looking for the green light at the end of somebody else's dock — and I found it on a forum, glowing in a dark-mode theme, with a post count beside it. Like Gatsby, I believed in the orgastic future that year by year recedes before us. Unlike Gatsby, my future arrived as a chargeback.

What follows is a collection of forum posts written in a previous life: the marginalia of a young man who had read just enough Nietzsche to think he was beyond good and evil, and not nearly enough Dostoevsky to know how that story ends. Raskolnikov had his theory of the extraordinary man, too. Everyone in the selva oscura has a theory.

What This Is

This repository is a historical archive — a preserved record of how I thought when I was living a "life of crime." It is kept the way one keeps an old journal, or the way a museum keeps a fossil: not because the creature should be brought back to life, but because it is instructive to see what it once looked like.

Consider it a primary source. Foucault would call it an archaeology of a particular mindset; Augustine would call it a Confessions without the conversion scene properly written yet. I call it a record of the person I was — preserved, annotated by time, and not endorsed by the person I am.

Socrates held that no one does wrong willingly, only out of ignorance. Reading these posts back, I'm inclined to agree — though I'd add that ignorance can be remarkably confident when it has a forum signature.

What This Is Not
It is not a tutorial. Nothing here should be read as instruction, and nothing here is offered as such.
It is not current. These methods are almost certainly expired. The systems they describe have been patched, deprecated, monitored, machine-learned against, and regulated out of existence many times over. Heraclitus said you cannot step into the same river twice; in fraud prevention, the river is rebuilt every quarter with better sensors.
It is not financial or legal advice. It is barely advice of any kind.
On Carding

Carding is dead.

Let this be stated plainly, without allegory. Between EMV chips, 3-D Secure, tokenization, real-time behavioral risk scoring, device fingerprinting, and the sheer coordination of issuers, processors, and law enforcement, the world those old posts describe no longer exists. Whatever romantic notion the forums once sold — and they sold it well — was always Ozymandias in waiting:

"Look on my Works, ye Mighty, and despair!" Nothing beside remains.

What remains are the cautionary tales, the indictments, and the people who learned the hard way that the house doesn't just always win — the house writes the rules, keeps the logs, and has subpoena power.

Why Keep It Public?

Because burning the diary doesn't change who wrote it. Because honesty about where you came from is worth more than a curated history. And because, as Kierkegaard put it, life can only be understood backwards, though it must be lived forwards.

This is me understanding it backwards.

Epilogue

So we beat on, boats against the current — though in my case, I eventually turned the boat around.

Today I build software, test systems, and spend my time on things I can put my real name on. If you're here out of curiosity, welcome to the museum. If you're here looking for a shortcut: there isn't one, there never really was, and the ones that looked like shortcuts were just longer roads with worse endings.
